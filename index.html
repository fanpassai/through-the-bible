THE PROMISE · GENESIS 12

God calls Abram and gives him something to trust.

God tells Abram to leave his country and go to a land He will show him. The promise can be remembered with three handles: Land. Seed. Blessing.

TAP REVEAL

Next: The promise has been spoken. But Abraham still has no child. That tension takes us to Genesis 15.
GENESIS 15 · TRUST BEFORE SIGHT

Years pass. Abraham still has no child.

Abraham brings that problem before God. God takes him outside, points him to the stars, and tells him that his descendants will be beyond what he can presently see.

What changed? Nothing visible yet. But Abraham believed God. The promise is now moving from words toward a family.
THE FAMILY · FROM ABRAHAM TO ISRAEL

The promise becomes a family.

Abraham had no son, and he brought that ache honestly to God. God answered by taking him beneath the stars and promising descendants beyond what Abraham could count or see.

Then the promised son finally came. Isaac was born when Abraham was 100 years old and Sarah was 90. Isaac is not a random new character; he is the visible arrival of the promise Abraham and Sarah had waited to receive.

Isaac has a son named Jacob. God later renames Jacob Israel. Israel has twelve sons, whose families become the tribes of Israel.

JOSEPH · WHY EGYPT?

Joseph explains the move.

Joseph was one of Jacob’s sons, and his brothers hated him. Their jealousy became betrayal: they sold him to traders who were going down to Egypt. Joseph arrived there as a slave, far from home and far from the family story he knew.

But Joseph’s story did not end as a slave. Through suffering, faithfulness, and God’s care, Joseph eventually rose into authority in Egypt. When famine came, his position became the way Jacob’s family survived and came to live in Egypt.
